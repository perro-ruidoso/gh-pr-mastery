The Method

Four questions, asked in order, that work on any repository — and the one rule that keeps your answers honest.

M06 · Evidence Bloom: Analyze Assessed by: A1 (written)

Objective: Read the course repo's own issue graph and PR history as an exhibit and describe the workflow that produced it.

1. What is the unit of work?

Does one PR correspond to one issue, or do PRs arrive unlinked? Count how many merged PRs close an issue:

$ gh pr list -R OWNER/REPO --state merged --limit 30 \
    --json number,title,closingIssuesReferences \
    --jq '[.[] | select(.closingIssuesReferences|length > 0)] | length'

A high ratio means work is specified before it is done. A low one means the issue tracker is decoration. Remember M04: --limit 30 is a sample, so say so when you report the number.

2. How big are the changes?

$ gh pr list -R OWNER/REPO --state merged --limit 30 \
    --json number,additions,deletions \
    --jq 'sort_by(-(.additions + .deletions)) | .[0:5] | .[] | "\(.number)\t\(.additions + .deletions)"'

The distribution tells you whether review is real. A repository whose median PR is 1,200 lines is not reviewing; it is approving.

3. Who contributes, and from where?

isCrossRepository separates maintainers from outside contributors. A repo with no fork PRs has a closed contribution model; one with many has a triage burden, which is exactly what Week 6 puts you in the seat for.

$ gh pr list -R OWNER/REPO --state merged --limit 30 \
    --json isCrossRepository \
    --jq 'group_by(.isCrossRepository) | map({fork: .[0].isCrossRepository, count: length})'

4. What gates did it pass?

$ gh pr view NNN -R OWNER/REPO --json reviewDecision,statusCheckRollup,mergedBy

Was it approved, and by someone other than the author? Did checks pass before the merge or after? reviewDecision gives the review verdict (APPROVED, CHANGES_REQUESTED, REVIEW_REQUIRED, or empty when no review was required); statusCheckRollup lists each check with its conclusion; mergedBy names the account that pressed the button. Week 5 is about configuring these; today you only need to read them.

Infer carefully — absence of evidence is not evidence

A PR with no review comments may mean the change was trivial, or that it was discussed in Slack, or that review is a formality here. You can say "there is no recorded review discussion on this PR" with certainty. You cannot say "this change was not reviewed." Keep your claims to what the artifacts actually support — and say which artifact supports each one. That distinction is most of the A1 rubric.

Writing it up

The shape that earns marks is the two-column table from the previous page, in prose: each sentence carries its own evidence. Compare:

Unsupported

"The team reviews carefully and keeps PRs small."

Two claims, no artifacts. A grader cannot check either.

Supported

"Of the last 30 merged PRs, 27 have a non-empty closingIssuesReferences; the largest is 412 lines (additions + deletions, PR #88) and the median is 61. PR #90 shows reviewDecision: APPROVED with mergedBy a different login from author."

Every number names the field and the command that produced it.

Hands-on checklist

Run the method once on a repository you did not choose before you run it on the one you will write about.

Self-check

1. You find a merged PR with no review comments. What is the defensible statement?

C. Report the absence, not a motive. The repository records what happened in the repository; conversations in chat, pairing sessions, and hallway reviews leave no trace. Note also that a PR can carry a formal approval with no comments at all — check reviewDecision before concluding anything.

2. Why does a repository's PR-size distribution tell you something about its review culture?

A. Size is a proxy for reviewability, and reviewability is a precondition for review meaning anything. (B is a real UI behaviour but not the point; C is unsupportable — a mechanical rename can be enormous and trivial, which is why you read size alongside what changed; D is true and irrelevant.)

3. Which field tells you whether a PR carried a formal approval?

B. reviewDecision is the review verdict. statusCheckRollup is about automated checks, mergedBy is who merged, and isCrossRepository is where the head lives. Each answers exactly one question; the analysis goes wrong when one field is asked to answer another's.

4. Your "merged PRs that close an issue" query returns 30 out of 30. What is the honest way to report it?

D. Thirty is the --limit, not the population, so the claim is about a sample of the most recent PRs — and it is about a field, not about a habit. A is a claim about PRs you did not fetch; B misreads the limit as a count; C infers an intention no artifact records.

Flashcards

Say the answer out loud before you flip. "Again" puts the card back in the queue; "Got it" retires it. Keyboard: Space flips, 1 again, 2 got it.

Recite the four questions of the method, in order.
1. What is the unit of work? 2. How big are the changes? 3. Who contributes, and from where? 4. What gates did it pass?
Which field answers "what is the unit of work?", and what does a high ratio mean?
closingIssuesReferences on merged PRs. High ratio: work is specified before it is done. Low: the tracker is decoration.
What does a repository's PR-size distribution tell you?
Whether review is real. Past a certain size approval stops being review — a median of 1,200 lines "is not reviewing; it is approving."
Which field separates maintainers from outside contributors?
isCrossRepository. Many fork PRs means a triage burden (Week 6); none means a closed contribution model.
Name the three "gates" fields and what each answers.
reviewDecision (was it approved?), statusCheckRollup (did checks pass?), mergedBy (who merged — the author or someone else?).
"This PR has no review comments." What may you conclude?
Only that there is no recorded review discussion. Not that it was unreviewed — absence of evidence is not evidence.
Your query used --limit 30. How must every number you report from it be qualified?
As a sample of the 30 most recent matching items, not the population. Say the sample size with the ratio.
What makes a sentence in your write-up "supported"?
It names the artifact — a field, a PR number, a timestamp, a review thread — and ideally the command that produced it.
Ask your teacher

Before you write anything for A1, paste your draft paragraph into Claude Code and ask it to "underline every claim that does not name an artifact, and for each one suggest the gh command that would produce one." Then run the commands and revise. Bring anything still unclear to the weekly meeting.
